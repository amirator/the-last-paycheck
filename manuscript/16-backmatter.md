# Appendix: Study Summary Table

The ten studies this book leans on hardest, in one place. Full citations in the chapter notes.

| Study | Year | Data | What it found | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frey & Osborne, *Future of Employment* | 2013 | 702 occupations, expert ratings | 47% of US employment "at risk" of computerisation | Occupation-level; read as a forecast, wasn't one |
| Eloundou et al., *GPTs are GPTs* | 2023–24 | O\*NET tasks × model capability | ~80% of workers ≥10% tasks exposed; ~19% ≥50% | Exposure only; no employment outcomes |
| ILO–NASK refined global index | 2025 | Global task-level ratings | 1 in 4 jobs exposed globally; 34% in high-income; clerical most exposed | Potential exposure, not job loss |
| Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, *Canaries* | 2025–26 | ADP payroll, 3.5–5M workers/mo | 22–25s in exposed jobs 19% below kept-pace path; via hiring | Descriptive, not causal; ADP tilts large/exposed |
| Hosseini & Lichtinger | 2025 | ~62M résumés, 285k firms | Junior employment −9–10% at GenAI-adopting firms | Adoption proxy from postings |
| Humlum & Vestergaard | 2025 | Danish national data, 11 occupations | Precisely estimated ~null effects | Denmark ≠ US; early window |
| Anthropic Economic Index | 2025– | Millions of AI conversations | 57% augmentation vs 43% automation; usage concentrated | One company's models; early adopters |
| Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, *Generative AI at Work* | 2023–25 | 5,179 support agents, field experiment | +14% productivity avg; +34% novices; ~0 for experts | One firm, one occupation |
| Noy & Zhang (*Science*) | 2023 | 453 professionals, RCT | 40% faster, 18% higher quality writing | Lab tasks; no fact-checking dimension |
| Dell'Acqua et al., *Jagged Frontier* | 2023 | 758 BCG consultants, RCT | +12.2% tasks, +25.1% speed, ~40% quality inside frontier; −19 pp outside | Elite consultants; designed tasks |

# Appendix: The Decade Ahead, by the Numbers

What the US government's own projectors expect for 2024–2034. These are not forecasts of AI's effect alone (all drivers are combined), but they are the official baseline every claim in this debate should be checked against. Source: US Bureau of Labor Statistics, Employment Projections 2024–34 (released August 28, 2025); the BLS names AI adoption as a factor in several of the declines.

| Data entry keyers | −25.9% |
| Telemarketers | −22.1% |
| Procurement clerks | −8.7% |
