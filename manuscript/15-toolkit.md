# The Toolkit: What To Do Monday

Everything in this book compresses into one month of deliberate work. Four weeks, one move per week, each with a done-condition so you know when to stop. No step requires permission, a budget, or anyone finding out before you're ready.

## Week 1: Audit (the Chapter 11 worksheet, for real this time)

Reading about the worksheet is not doing the worksheet.

1. List your ten core tasks and hours, from your actual calendar and sent folder, not your job description.
2. Score each task: capability (0–2), presence (0–2), accountability (0–2), verification cost (0–2). For every task where you guessed on capability, stop guessing. Paste a real (confidentiality-safe) example into a current model and grade the output like a boss would.
3. Compute your exposure share: exposed hours ÷ total hours.

**Done when:** you have a one-page sheet with ten scored tasks, a percentage at the bottom, and the single exposed task that eats the most hours circled. The circling matters.

## Week 2: Learn the tool on your biggest exposed task

Not a course. Not a certification. One task, the circled one, done with the machine every single working day for a week.

1. Each day, do the circled task twice: your old way, and the machine-first way (model drafts, you finish). Time both.
2. Keep a frontier log, one line per day: where the model nailed it, where it was confidently wrong. By Friday you'll know where the jagged edge runs through your own work, which Chapter 8 says is the actual skill.
3. Save your best prompts and checklists as reusable templates.

**Done when:** the machine-first version of the circled task is at least twice as fast at equal quality, and your frontier log has five entries.

## Week 3: Produce one visible artifact

Chapter 6's lesson: if practice no longer comes attached to a paycheck, manufacture the evidence of judgment yourself. One artifact, finished, visible.

Pick by role. A before/after of a process you accelerated, with numbers from Week 2. A dashboard, template, or checklist your team actually adopts. A short written analysis of something your organization genuinely needs answered. The bar: someone senior could forward it without edits.

**Done when:** the artifact exists, is finished, and at least one person who could influence your next role has seen it.

## Week 4: Make the case

The gains of Chapter 8 went to workers; whether pay follows is an open question in aggregate. Individually, the premium goes to the people who claim it, and postings are advertising 28 percent more for AI-skilled roles.¹

1. Write the one-page memo: what you automated, hours saved, the artifact, and what you propose to own next. The checking, deploying, accountable layer. The judgment work above the tasks you've handed to the machine.
2. Deliver it internally, to your manager, skip-level, whoever owns your next move. If your employer has no next move for you, the same memo, lightly rewritten, is a portfolio piece aimed outward. The market for proof of judgment is bigger than your org chart.
3. Book the recurrence: a calendar entry six months out to re-run the Week 1 worksheet. Your score is dated; the frontier isn't finished.

**Done when:** the memo is sent (not drafted, sent) and the six-month re-audit is on the calendar.

Thirty days. An audit, a tool mastered where it hurts most, an artifact, a claim staked. None of it waits for policy, your employer's AI strategy, or the next model release.
